One Burning Heat Seeker

Find the heat. Follow the evidence.

The Fire Pack is the engagement. One burning question, one defined area of examination, and the evidence that can actually answer it. We identify what is known, what is documented, what is missing, inconsistent, unclear, or unsupported—and show you what the record actually says.

01

Define

Name the heat.

We start with the specific concern. Not “Are we compliant?” but the question precise enough to investigate.

02

Trace

Follow the record.

We examine the governing requirement, the relevant records, the actual statement or practice, and the evidence connecting them.

03

Test

Separate fact from assumption.

If the concern is substantiated, we show why. If information is missing, we identify the gap. If the evidence does not support the concern, that is the finding.

04

Return

Make the next move visible.

You receive a bounded finding, an evidence trail, the gaps that remain, and a practical indication of what needs to happen next.

The Fire Pack readout

This is not a fire drill.

The point is not to make ordinary uncertainty sound like an emergency. The point is to establish what is actually happening.

What we examine

The question sets the boundary.

The Fire Pack can examine a defined concern involving claims, labeling, product changes, documentation, requirement interpretation, known findings, or another question the available evidence can answer.

01

Claims & representations

What are you saying, and what supports the statement?

02

Labeling & packaging

Does the actual package align with the requirements that govern it?

03

Product changes

Did a change in formulation, format, ingredients, process, market, or presentation change the regulatory picture?

04

Documentation

Can the business produce the record necessary to support what it says it does?

05

Known findings

Has a regulator, customer, partner, internal reviewer, or other party already raised a specific concern?

The receipts matter.

No invented facts. No convenient assumptions. No “probably.”

Every finding has to connect back to something: a governing requirement, a submitted record, a product statement, a label, a procedure, a documented practice, a piece of evidence—or a clearly identified gap where the evidence should exist but doesn't.

If we cannot establish it from the available record, it stays unestablished.
