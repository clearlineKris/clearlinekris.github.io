Autobiography · professional edition

I somehow worked around almost the whole plant.

Since 2017, my route through regulated cannabis has run through the floor, inventory, security, documentation, compliance, and the systems connecting them. That means I know a lot about what happens before, after, around, and because of the plant-touching work — with two very specific exceptions.

01 · The floor

The rule is never the whole story.

Working inside regulated operations taught me that policy only becomes real when somebody has to use it while customers are waiting, inventory is moving, records are accumulating, and the day is refusing to behave like the SOP.

That is where my interest lives: the gap between what the rule says and what the operation has to prove happened.

02 · The record

I kept ending up where the evidence lives.

Inventory, security, documentation, and compliance all force the same question from different angles: what happened, what can you demonstrate, and what does the record actually support?

That question eventually became the through-line behind the tools I build now. I am less interested in making operations look complicated than in making complicated operations legible.

03 · The missing rooms

And then there are exactly two doors I never walked through.

I have worked adjacent to the full supply chain, including inside vertically integrated operations, but I have never directly been the person cultivating the flower or processing concentrates.

Marginal proficiencies: Cultivating Buds — Processing Dabs.

That is not something I want hidden behind a broad “cannabis experience” label. It is useful information. Knowing where direct experience stops is part of knowing what any claim is worth.

04 · Why this matters

The gaps sharpen the work.

Because I came up through handoffs, records, controls, and downstream consequences, I tend to see operations as systems rather than isolated jobs. I want to understand what each team needs from the one before it, what proof survives the handoff, and where unnecessary friction gets mistaken for compliance.

Know what you know. Name what you do not. Build from the evidence between them.
