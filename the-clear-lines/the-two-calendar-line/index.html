The Two-Calendar Line

A federal extension can move one deadline while a state deadline stays exactly where it was. Your inventory has to survive both calendars.

Live · October 2026HempFederal lawState law
The line

A federal delay does not move every state clock.

← Back to the floor

Regulatory status note: Current through October 14, 2026. The federal timetable and the New Jersey and Minnesota examples below were checked against the linked law and regulator guidance.

First, the federal calendar

Section 2019 of Public Law 119-103 delays most of Section 781’s hemp-definition changes until December 11, 2026. The exclusions for intermediate and final products containing cannabinoids that the plant cannot naturally produce still apply November 12. The delayed provisions include the broader total-THC framework and the finished-container ceiling above 0.4 mg combined THC and specified similar-effect cannabinoids.

The separate exclusion for naturally producible cannabinoids synthesized or manufactured outside the plant is also part of the December timetable. “Synthetic” is too loose a shortcut for choosing the November date. Read the actual category. The Hemp Line explains what changes at each layer.

Now, the state calendar

New Jersey has a separate transition. Its law sets November 13, 2026 as the deadline for sales of intoxicating hemp beverages under the temporary pathway. The Cannabis Regulatory Commission’s guidance explains that beverages above 0.4 mg total THC per container must enter the state’s licensed cannabis manufacturing and retail system after that transition. Existing age, testing, and potency conditions apply during it.

December 11 is not an extra month of New Jersey permission. A federal classification does not create a state retail license.

A different example: Minnesota

Minnesota OCM says the federal definition change does not itself amend Minnesota’s lower-potency hemp edible definitions, products, or license types. State authorization and federal classification can therefore give different answers about the same product. Passing one test does not settle the other.

These two examples show why a fifty-state deadline list can mislead. The question is which law covers this product, this activity, and this location—not which date has the biggest headline.

Turn dates into decisions

For each product, record the cannabinoid and production process, test evidence, finished-container amount, sales jurisdiction, license pathway, and date each applicable rule changes. Identify who owns the decision to stop making, transferring, or selling it if the pathway closes.

A bill being introduced is not a date being extended. Preserve the enacted text and the current state guidance behind the decision.

Two calendars. One shelf. Check both before stocking it.
