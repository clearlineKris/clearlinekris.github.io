The Pause Line

A hearing can finish without the case being finished. A response deadline can pass without a rule changing. Federal process has more than one kind of “done.”

The line

A filing, an order, and a final rule are different events.

Start with the document type

A proposal tells the public what an agency is considering. A hearing develops an administrative record. A party’s motion asks for action. A judge’s procedural order sets what happens next. A final agency action has a different job.

Confusing those documents can turn a headline into an operating instruction that no authority actually issued.

What the current record says

The broader marijuana rescheduling hearing ran from June 29 through July 15. On September 29, the judge stayed the matter while considering a request to admit a GAO report and permit supplemental briefing.

The October 14 order confirms that the government filed its opposition October 9 and other parties filed support October 13. New motions ask to reply and to reopen the hearing for limited discovery and testimony. The judge allows the reply, reserves the decision on admitting the report, and requires the government’s response to reopening by October 21. The matter remains stayed.

A deadline for a response is not a date for a final rescheduling decision.

What to watch next

Use the DEA docket page to look for the response and a later order resolving the motions. Read the operative language: what was granted, what was denied, what remains reserved, and whether the stay continues.

After the hearing stage, a recommended decision and record can still be followed by exceptions and agency review. The exact next step depends on the governing procedure and later orders. Do not invent another mandatory proposal-and-comment round, a completion date, or a court outcome.

Keep the two tracks separate

This stay concerns the broader proceeding. The April medical scheduling rule is a separate action already in effect. An order about adding evidence does not, by itself, undo that rule. Read The Schedule Line for the distinction.

For a working record, capture the document, issue date, proceeding, action ordered, response deadline, affected activities, and next review trigger. Keep predictions in a different column from decisions.

Read what the order does before guessing where the case goes.
