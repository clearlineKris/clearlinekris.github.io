The Schedule Line

Schedule III is a classification. To understand what changed, first ask which marijuana, which activity, and which federal track.

Live · October 2026Federal lawMedical cannabisDEA
The line

One medical rule in force. One broader case still open.

← Back to the floor

Regulatory status note: Current through October 14, 2026. The October 14 DEA order keeps the broader case stayed and sets an October 21 response deadline.

Which track are we talking about?

“Marijuana was rescheduled” leaves out the part an operator actually needs. DOJ’s April 2026 action moved FDA-approved marijuana products and marijuana covered by qualifying state medical licenses into Schedule III. Marijuana outside those categories remains in Schedule I. Adult-use authorization does not, by itself, put a product into the medical category.

The final rule took effect April 28, 2026. A separate proceeding considers broader rescheduling. An action in one track is not automatically an action in the other.

What does a state medical license do?

It identifies a state pathway. Federal operation still has conditions. The rule creates an expedited DEA registration process for state medical licensees, with controls tied to the activity and premises. California DCC directs medicinal operators to the DEA registration portal. A state license, a submitted application, and a granted federal registration are different records.

The rule’s temporary permission for applications submitted within 60 days of publication is a specific transition provision. Do not assume a newly submitted application carries that protection. Check the current instructions and the application’s actual date and status.

What is paused?

The hearing in the broader case ran June 29–July 15. A September 29 stay allowed briefing over whether a GAO report should enter the record. The October 14 order records the government’s October 9 opposition and supporting responses filed October 13. It reserves a decision on admitting the report and orders the government to respond to a motion to reopen the hearing by October 21. The case remains stayed.

That is a procedural pause in the broader case, not a statement that the April medical rule has been reversed. The Pause Line explains how to read the docket.

What reaches the operating floor?

Treasury describes a change to Section 280E treatment for activities removed from Schedule I or II, while allocation and transition questions await guidance. State packaging obligations also remain, with an additional federal patient-warning requirement where applicable.

Keep the medical and adult-use activities, license records, registration status, product identity, and transaction evidence distinct. Read What Schedule III Won’t Change before turning a classification into a promise.
