The Schedule Line

Medical and adult-use marijuana no longer occupy the same federal scheduling lane.

Live · October 2026
The line

Medical and adult-use marijuana no longer occupy the same federal scheduling lane.

← Back to the floor

Regulatory review: October 18, 2026. Sources are linked beside the relevant claims. Unverified later procedural outcomes are identified explicitly.

01 — THE SPLIT

The rule effective April 28, 2026 moved covered FDA-approved marijuana products and marijuana subject to a state medical marijuana license to Schedule III. Other marijuana remains Schedule I. A medical label alone does not establish which federal rules cover a transaction.

02 — REGISTRATION STILL MATTERS

The DEA regulatory-actions page provides the medical dispensary registration portal. The rule created an expedited route for state medical license holders; it did not turn every state license into a federal registration. California DCC says applications remain open, but those submitted after June 26 are outside the initial expedition provision.

03 — THE BROADER PROCEEDING

The broader rescheduling process is separate from the effective medical rule. An October 8 report by Ogletree says the ALJ paused it on September 29 to consider a GAO report, with agency responses due October 13. This review did not locate a later order confirming what happened after that deadline. Do not infer that the pause ended or that broader rescheduling became final.

04 — TAX IS A SEPARATE QUESTION

Section 280E generally bars deductions and credits for trafficking in Schedule I or II substances. Treasury says the medical rescheduling action generally removes that bar for qualifying activities; allocation and transition guidance remains a pending item in the 2026–2027 IRS guidance plan. The SAFE Banking Act of 2026 was reintroduced in June; it is proposed legislation, not an enacted banking safe harbor.

05 — CHECK THE ACTIVITY

Identify the product, license designation, federal registration position, and transaction. Keep medical and adult-use records clear enough to explain their different treatment. Read What Schedule III Won’t Change before treating a scheduling change as permission for a new activity.
